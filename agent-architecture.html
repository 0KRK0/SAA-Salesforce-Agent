Architecture reference · August 2026

One agent.
Thirty-eight tools.

What the Salesforce agent actually connects to, what it is allowed to do, and why it is not a fleet of agents.

The three short answers
Are we using MCP? No — not today.

Zero MCP servers are configured. Every tool reaches Salesforce through its own REST, Metadata, Tooling, Bulk and Analytics APIs. MCP support exists as an optional extra a customer can switch on.

What do we get access to? The whole org — through one user.

An OAuth token scoped to api and refresh_token, acting as the Salesforce user who connected. Their permissions are the ceiling.

How many agents? One.

A single reasoning loop with thirty-eight tools, grouped into ten capability areas. Those areas are tool families, not separate agents.

How the agent reaches Salesforce

There are two possible routes out of the runtime. Only one of them is carrying traffic. Both, critically, pass through the same gate — which is what stops MCP from becoming a way around the safety layer.

Why direct APIs rather than MCP

MCP is a transport. It does not give you anything the Salesforce APIs don't already expose, and it takes away the two things this product is built on: deterministic control over exactly which call is made, and the ability to verify a change by reading the org back. A tool that deploys metadata and then re-retrieves it to confirm cannot be a passthrough.

What the agent is actually allowed to do

"Access to the whole org" is true but incomplete. There are five independent ceilings between a person typing a sentence and a record changing, and the narrowest one wins. This is the part worth being able to explain in a security review.

The honest caveat for your sales conversations

Metadata work — creating fields, deploying Flows, shipping Apex — requires Modify All Data or Customize Application on the connected user. There is no way around that; it is what those APIs demand. So in practice a customer who wants the engineering capabilities is connecting an admin-grade user, and the four ceilings you control become the ones that actually matter.

"How many agents" — the real answer

One. There is a single runtime, a single Claude loop, a single approval queue and a single audit trail. What the roadmap calls the "Flow agent" or the "data quality agent" are tool families — a group of tools plus the services behind them. They do not have their own reasoning loops and do not call each other.

This is worth saying plainly, because it is a strength that is easy to describe as a weakness. Ten capability areas behind one gate is one place to audit and one place to fail. Ten autonomous agents would be ten.

Where MCP fits — and where it doesn't

Salesforce made their own Hosted MCP Servers generally available in April 2026, so this is a live question rather than a hypothetical. It is worth knowing exactly what they do, because the answer is good news for your positioning.

The line worth remembering

Salesforce's MCP servers let an AI operate an org. They do not let it build one. Everything in the lower half of that table — fields, Flows, Apex, dependencies, releases — is the engineering layer, and it is the part a Salesforce team actually pays a consultant for. That is the business you are in.

Which means MCP is complementary, not competitive, and the way it is already wired is the right way: a customer who wants their agent to also reach Jira, or a Salesforce MCP server, or an internal system, registers it — and it lands behind the same gate as everything else. You are not choosing between MCP and direct APIs. You are choosing direct APIs for the work you are accountable for, and MCP for the reach you are not.

If you are building a company on this
  • Say "one agent, thirty-eight tools." It is accurate, and it survives a technical diligence conversation. "Twelve agents" does not, and the moment someone opens the repo the claim costs you more than it bought.
  • Lead with the gate, not the capability list. Every competitor can claim to create a field. The differentiator is that a human approved this exact change, the approval expired in an hour, and the org was re-read to prove it landed.
  • The admin-credential requirement is your biggest objection. Get ahead of it: sandbox-first, production mutations off by default, two approvers for anything touching security. Those defaults are already in the product — make them part of the pitch.
  • Watch Salesforce's MCP roadmap. Today it stops at operating an org. If it ever reaches metadata authoring, the ground shifts — and the thing that would still be yours is the safety and release machinery, not the API access.
